**Response (Facts & Details)**:
> Facial harmony refers to the visual balance between dental features and the rest of the face. It evaluates how teeth interact with surrounding structures such as the lips, nose, chin, jawline, and cheeks. Rather than aiming for perfect symmetry, facial harmony focuses on proportion, flow, and natural transitions. In aesthetic dentistry, minor asymmetries are often preserved as they contribute to realism — overcorrecting can result in smiles that appear artificial or disconnected from the face.
> Key principles include: symmetry and proportion — using guidelines like the golden ratio (approximately 1.618) for width relationships between upper front teeth, while slight balanced asymmetry often looks more natural; smile line and tooth display — the curve of upper teeth should follow the curve of the upper lip, with appropriate gum display; gum and lip balance — even, symmetrical gum lines matching lip shape, with lip dynamics considered for proper support. Different face shapes require different approaches: oval faces allow greater flexibility; round faces benefit from slightly longer or more angular teeth; square faces suit softer, rounded tooth edges; long faces need wider tooth proportions; and heart-shaped faces benefit from fuller anterior teeth.
